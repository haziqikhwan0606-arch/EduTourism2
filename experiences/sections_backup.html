THE WAY WE LEARN

Learning happens everywhere.

We believe meaningful learning extends beyond traditional classrooms. By experiencing the world directly, participants gain new perspectives, connect knowledge with reality and develop a deeper understanding of the people, places and environments around them.

01
STAGE 01

OBSERVE

See the world from a different perspective.

Participants observe real-world environments, identify key features and examine how places, communities and industries operate.

Field observation Discovery Awareness Critical thinking
02
STAGE 02

EXPLORE

Go beyond what you already know.

Participants explore different environments, cultures, industries and destinations through structured visits and experiential activities.

Exploration Discovery Experiential learning Practical exposure
03
STAGE 03

INTERACT

Learn through people and shared experiences.

Participants engage with local students, communities, professionals and relevant stakeholders to exchange knowledge and perspectives.

Cross-cultural communication Collaboration Networking Knowledge exchange
04
STAGE 04

UNDERSTAND

Turn experiences into meaningful knowledge.

Participants reflect on their observations, connect experiences with academic concepts and communicate their findings through discussion and presentations.

Reflection Analysis Knowledge application Recommendations

DISCOVER MALAYSIA

One destination. Countless ways to learn.

From dynamic cities and cultural heritage to tropical landscapes and evolving industries, Malaysia offers a diverse environment for learning through exploration, observation and meaningful engagement.

Reference: Putrajaya & PETRONAS Twin Towers
URBAN ENVIRONMENTS

Cities & Architecture

Understanding how places are designed and developed.

Explore the relationship between architecture, infrastructure, urban planning and the communities that shape modern cities.

✦ Urban planning and city development
✦ Architecture and engineering structures
✦ Public spaces and waterfront development
✦ Infrastructure and built environments
✦ The relationship between cities and communities
LEARNING THEMES
Urban planning Architecture Engineering Public-space design
Reference: Living Heritage Communities & Historical Centers
CULTURAL DIVERSITY & HERITAGE

Culture & Heritage

Discovering the identity of a diverse nation.

Experience the pluralistic harmony of Malaysia through living heritage, community interaction, indigenous customs and multi-ethnic traditions.

✦ Malaysian cultural diversity & coexistence
✦ Local traditions and living heritage
✦ Community interaction and dialogue
✦ Cultural practices and everyday life
✦ Cross-cultural understanding & international exchange
LEARNING THEMES
Cultural awareness Heritage appreciation Community dialogue International exchange
Reference: Taman Negara National Park & Tropical Ecosystems
TROPICAL ECOSYSTEMS & CONSERVATION

Nature & Sustainability

Exploring the relationship between people and the environment.

Journey into some of the oldest rainforests on earth to study conservation, biodiversity stewardship and balanced environmental responsibility.

✦ Environmental awareness & ecological balance
✦ Conservation, rainforest habitats and biodiversity
✦ Sustainable development practices in nature reserves
✦ Natural environments and indigenous forest knowledge
✦ Social and environmental responsibility
LEARNING THEMES
Environmental education Conservation awareness Biodiversity Sustainability
Reference: Sky Ladder Farm Lukut, PKPKM & UCAM Alor Gajah

✦ Professional operations and smallholder practices

Agriculture Industry exposure Professional exposure Economic understanding
FEATURED INTERNATIONAL PROGRAMME

Malaysia Field Learning Programme 2027

Proposed 2027 Pilot for the University of Osaka, Japan

An international field learning experience designed to connect Japanese and Malaysian students through collaborative exploration, industry exposure, cultural understanding and real-world learning in Malaysia.

Proposed pilot. Subject to institutional discussion, mutual agreement and formal confirmation.
Programme MFLP 2027
Duration 6 Days, 5 Nights
International Cohort 12–16 Osaka Undergrads
Local Peer Participants 4–6 IUC Students
Coordinator Olympia Education Malaysia
Proposed Academic Host IUC Malaysia
PROGRAMME FOUNDATION

Three Core Learning Pillars

PILLAR 01

URBAN PLANNING

Understanding the development of modern cities.

  • Urban planning and waterfront development
  • Public-space design & human-centric infrastructure
  • Urban environment & ecological water management
  • Relationship between built environments and communities
Proposed Location: Putrajaya Waterfront
PILLAR 02

ENGINEERING & ARCHITECTURE

Exploring the intersection of design, technology and engineering.

  • Modern architectural design & iconic forms
  • Building structures and mega-foundations
  • Skybridge engineering and observation mechanics
  • Public-space design and urban development
Proposed Location: PETRONAS Twin Towers
PILLAR 03

AGRICULTURE & SUSTAINABILITY

Understanding agricultural practices and economic contributions.

  • Pineapple cultivation and farm management
  • Agricultural tourism and community engagement
  • Oil palm smallholding practices with PKPKM
  • Rubber cultivation and agricultural heritage
Locations: Sky Ladder Farm Lukut, PKPKM, UCAM Alor Gajah
A SIX-DAY JOURNEY THROUGH MALAYSIA

Proposed Programme Itinerary Framework

6 days, 5 nights · Proposed itinerary activities, subject to formal institutional coordination.

DAY 01 · ARRIVAL & PROGRAMME INTRODUCTION

Welcome to Malaysia & Team Orientation

Arrival of Japanese participants in Malaysia. Welcome by Olympia Education and IUC.

Proposed Activities:
  • Arrival of Japanese participants in Malaysia
  • Welcome by Olympia Education and IUC
  • Introduction to programme objectives
  • Briefing on activities, learning approach and participant responsibilities
Learning Focus: Programme orientation and preparation for collaborative field learning.
DAY 02 · ACADEMIC ORIENTATION & URBAN PLANNING

Campus Orientation & Putrajaya Waterfront Field Study

Academic orientation at IUC followed by structured learning visits to Putrajaya.

Proposed Activities:
  • Orientation at IUC
  • Introduction to the institutional environment
  • Interaction with local students
  • Learning visit to Putrajaya waterfront
Learning Focus: Urban planning, public-space development and management of the urban environment.
DAY 03 · ARCHITECTURE & URBAN ENGINEERING

PETRONAS Twin Towers & KL Built Environment

Direct inspection of world-renowned architectural engineering and high-density design.

Proposed Activities:
  • Proposed visit to PETRONAS Twin Towers
  • Observation of the building structure
  • Introduction to the Skybridge
  • Exploration of the surrounding public-space design
Learning Focus: Architecture, engineering, design, technology and urban development.
DAY 04 · PINEAPPLE AGRICULTURE & PALM OIL INDUSTRY

Sky Ladder Farm & Oil Palm Smallholdings

Experiencing sustainable agricultural practices, agro-tourism and smallholder cooperatives.

Proposed Activities:
  • Proposed visit to Sky Ladder Pineapple Farm, Lukut
  • Introduction to pineapple cultivation
  • Learning about farm management and agricultural tourism
  • Proposed afternoon visit to an oil palm smallholding with PKPKM
Learning Focus: Agricultural practices, smallholder operations and the agricultural landscape.
DAY 05 · RUBBER INDUSTRY & AGRICULTURAL HERITAGE

UCAM Alor Gajah & Natural Rubber Heritage

Understanding agricultural heritage and commodity evolution in Malaysian economic history.

Proposed Activities:
  • Proposed visit to UCAM, Alor Gajah
  • Observation of a rubber-tapping demonstration
  • Introduction to the history and traditions of natural rubber cultivation
Learning Focus: Rubber cultivation, agricultural heritage and economic development.
DAY 06 · PRESENTATIONS & PROGRAMME CLOSING

Team Presentations, Feedback & Programme Closing

Academic reflection, collaborative team outputs and formal evaluation symposium.

Proposed Activities:
  • Return to IUC
  • Team presentations of learning outcomes
  • Sharing of observations, findings and recommendations
  • Feedback and evaluation session
  • Preparation for departure from Malaysia
Learning Focus: Reflection, knowledge sharing and communicating learning outcomes.
CROSS-BORDER COLLABORATION

Different perspectives. One shared learning experience.

The programme proposes bringing together students from Japan and Malaysia in collaborative mixed teams to examine real-world challenges.

3–4 Osaka Undergraduates per team
1–2 IUC Student Peers per team
Shared Field observations & discussions
01
One-page Field Brief

Each team prepares a concise document summarising its key observations and findings from the programme.

02
Team Recommendation

Each team develops at least one recommendation informed by its learning experience and presents it at IUC.

Academic Coordination

Discussion with Prof. Yoshinori Sumimura is proposed to review: curriculum alignment, student cohort size and composition, potential 2027 programme dates, and appointment of a university contact person.

Note: Proposed academic discussion, subject to formal mutual agreement.
